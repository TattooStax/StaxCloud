# Fargo Tattoo — Website Positioning Audit
### fargotattoo.com, reviewed firsthand on November 3, 2026

Every page was read directly: home, both artist pages, FAQ and policies, the request page, the client experiences page, the artist-choice article, and the booking page the site sends visitors to.

---

## 1. How the studio positions itself today

The site has one clear idea, and it repeats it everywhere: **you get matched with the artist who fits your idea.** The home page headline is "Fargo Tattoo. Two artists. The right fit for the idea," and the first paragraph says clients "are matched with the artist whose strengths best fit their idea." The FAQ says the same thing in the same words, and there is a whole article built on it ("How to choose the right Fargo Tattoo artist for your idea").

Around that core, the studio presents itself as:

- **Private and appointment-focused** — "a positive, professional, private tattoo studio," tattoo-only, inside Standout Salon at 1537 University Dr S.
- **Radically up-front about money and rules** — the rate, minimum, deposit, touch-up policy, and minor policy are all published in plain language (see Strengths).
- **Honest to a fault** — the site says more than once what it will *not* do: "The studio does not promise rankings, awards, or a guaranteed booking," and reviews are never copied or invented (see Weaknesses — this cuts both ways).

That is a genuinely distinctive position for a tattoo studio. Most shop websites sell flash and attitude; this one sells fit and honesty.

## 2. How each artist is positioned

**Shawn Lowe** — the established hand. His page opens "Owner and tattoo artist / Accepting bookings" and describes him as "Known for black and grey work, portraits, memorials, cover-ups, lettering, and custom design planned for long-term readability." His selling points are experience (about eight years tattooing, nearly four in Fargo–Moorhead), a deep portfolio (roughly 45 pieces shown, including a dedicated healed-work section), and candor: "Will refer a client when another artist is better suited."

**Leni B.** — the emerging color artist. Her page opens "Tattoo artist / New artist at Fargo Tattoo" and describes "bold color, clean shapes, and a playful neo-traditional and illustrative edge." She works Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday, 12–8. The strongest line on her page is Shawn's endorsement: "Leni's color instincts are unusually strong for an artist at the beginning of her professional career."

**The split between them is stated crisply** in the FAQ: "Choose Shawn for black and grey, portraits, memorials, cover-ups, and lettering. Choose Leni when the idea fits her approved color and illustrative work." A visitor who reads the FAQ or the artist-choice article cannot be confused about who does what. That clarity is the site's best asset.

## 3. What's strong

- **One position, said the same way everywhere.** Home page, FAQ, both artist pages, and the choice article all tell the same matching story. That consistency is rare and it is what makes a two-artist studio feel like a studio instead of two people sharing a room.
- **Pricing transparency most shops won't match.** "$120/hour standard rate · $100 minimum · $50 deposit" sits right on the home page. The FAQ states the deposit "is non-refundable. It may be transferred when rescheduling with at least 48 hours' notice," explains the touch-up policy in plain words (30 days to get on the books, hands and feet called out as high-fade), and publishes the minor policy with its legal citations. This is trust, printed.
- **Real work, labeled by artist.** Every portfolio piece carries the artist's name and style — six on the home page split evenly, deep galleries on each artist page, and a "100% healed work" section for Shawn. Proof instead of promises.
- **Cover-ups treated as a specialty, not a side note.** "Honest cover-up planning based on the existing ink, placement, and a stronger new design" is a named service, with its own FAQ entries and article. Few studios market their honesty about cover-ups; this one does, and it fits the brand.
- **Local grounding.** The address, the inside-Standout-Salon detail, and the service-area towns (Fargo, Moorhead, West Fargo, Dilworth, Horace) are named plainly.
- **Nothing fabricated.** "Written reviews stay on the Google Business Profile so nothing is invented or copied." The instinct is right — see the recommendation below for how to keep the honesty and lose the invisibility.

## 4. What's weak or missing

- **The booking page contradicts the website on the consultation price.** The FAQ says "Consultations are 30 minutes and cost $20." The booking page the site sends everyone to titles it "30-Minute Tattoo Consultation — $40 Flat Rate," prices it at $40, and then says in its own description "The $20 consultation fee covers the artist's scheduled time." Three numbers, one product. The booking page also carries typos a visitor will notice: a heading that reads "30 minute consolation Tuesdays only," a "For Hour Session," a stray letter in the deposit text, and a 2-hour session priced "240" with no dollar sign. (The "Tuesdays only" limit, if real, is also mentioned nowhere on the website itself.)
- **Saturday walk-ins are invisible — and they start November 7.** The word "walk-in" appears nowhere on the site. The closest the FAQ gets is "Same-day appointments may be available; call or text ahead to ask about an opening." Everything on the site, including Leni's page, frames the studio as appointment-only ("Appointments preferred," "appointment-focused"), so a walk-in customer has no reason to believe the door is open to them on Saturdays.
- **One rate is presented as the studio rate, with no word that Leni's pricing is her own.** The home page fact card, the FAQ ("The standard rate is $120 per hour"), and the booking page all quote a single rate for the whole studio. If Leni's prices differ from Shawn's, a visitor who books her through the site has been quoted his rate by default — a surprise waiting at the chair.
- **Asking for Leni takes more effort than asking for Shawn.** The request page explains "This form does not send email directly": a Shawn request opens a text draft, a Leni request opens an *email draft* to her address, and the visitor still has to press send in their own app. Leni's own page does say "text the studio and ask for Leni," but the form itself routes her requests to the higher-friction path. Every extra step costs real requests, and the cost lands on the newer artist.
- **A visitor never sees a single review number.** The client experiences page spends most of its words explaining why reviews are *not* shown ("This page does not invent quotes, does not copy Google review text…"). The reasoning is sound, but the effect is defensive: no star rating, no review count, nothing a first-time visitor can weigh. For a studio whose reputation is its position, the proof is one click further away than it needs to be.
- **The "Why Fargo Tattoo" section talks to the industry, not to clients.** Its opening is: "Talent can develop through different paths. Professional opportunity still has to be earned through artwork, discipline, safety, integrity, accountability, and continued learning." That's a standards manifesto. A client deciding where to get tattooed gets nothing from it — while the section's own heading, "Honest matching. Private studio," already says the client-facing version better.
- **Leni's page leads with her biography instead of her work.** Before a single portfolio piece, a visitor reads that she is new, self-taught, and was "turned away by other shops." The story is honest and human, but it asks the visitor to take a chance; her work and Shawn's endorsement ask them to book. The order should be flipped. Related: her specialties list includes "Selected fine-line blackwork," which blurs the clean split the rest of the site works hard to draw (fine line is Shawn's side of the line).

## 5. Recommendations, in priority order

1. **Fix the consultation price everywhere, today.** Pick the real number and make the FAQ, the booking page title, its description, and its price all say it. While there, fix the booking page typos ("consolation," "For Hour Session," the bare "240") — that page is where money decisions happen, and the home page lists "Clear pricing" as a reason to choose the studio. Right now the site's own booking page undercuts that claim.
2. **Put Saturday walk-ins on the site before November 7.** One line in three places: the home page (near the hours), Leni's page (next to "Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday"), and the FAQ (a "Do you take walk-ins?" entry). Name the terms as they will actually run — Saturdays, smaller pieces, first come first served, with Leni — so the page creates the expectation the studio intends to keep.
3. **Give Leni's pricing one honest sentence.** Wherever the $120 rate appears as a studio-wide fact, add that Leni sets her own rates and hers are confirmed when the studio reviews the request — or state her rate if it's settled. Anything beats a visitor learning at the chair that the rate they read doesn't apply.
4. **Make requesting Leni as easy as requesting Shawn.** Let the request form route her requests by text ("text the studio and ask for Leni," as her page already offers) with email as the backup, and until then, say plainly on the form what will happen when they submit — the current wording explains the mechanism ("opens an email draft") instead of the outcome.
5. **Show the Google rating, keep the honesty.** The reviews page can link the live Google listing *and* state the rating and review count as they stand on Google, dated, without copying a single review — the same "nothing invented" standard, but with the number visible. A studio that ranks first for its own name but is hard to find on a generic "tattoo shop in Fargo" search (an October 2 check found exactly that, with review count the clearest gap against competitors — not re-verified today) cannot afford to hide the proof it has.
6. **Rewrite the "Why Fargo Tattoo" opening in client language.** Keep the standards, translate the benefit: what earned-opportunity discipline means for the person in the chair is a vetted artist, honest fit, and work planned to hold up — the page already has that language elsewhere ("tattoos planned to stay readable," "no false promises"). Lead with it; move the manifesto below or cut it.
7. **Reorder Leni's page: work first, endorsement second, story third.** Open with her strongest color pieces and Shawn's "unusually strong color instincts" line, then her days and how to request her, then the persistence story for readers who want it. And drop "Selected fine-line blackwork" from her specialties unless it's a deliberate offer — the site's whole position rests on the two artists *not* overlapping.
